# Horizons lesson archetypes

Horizons does not use one page template for every lesson. It uses a controlled family of spread archetypes. All archetypes obey the same exercise-flow rule: numbered exercises remain one vertical sequence on each page.

## 1. Standard language spread

Best for grammar/vocabulary lessons with several short tasks.

Typical rhythm:

- Page 1: lesson header → context/input → vocabulary → first controlled task
- Page 2: language focus → controlled practice → listening/pronunciation → communication

Suggested density: medium.

## 2. Photo-led vocabulary spread

Best for possessions, clothes, food, places, travel, weather, objects.

Typical rhythm:

- large hero or matching strip;
- vocabulary elicitation/matching;
- short listening or reading;
- personalization.

Suggested density: light to medium.

## 3. Reading feature spread

Best for real-world articles, interviews, diaries, blogs, travel stories and profiles.

Typical rhythm:

- Page 1 dominated by one large feature reading;
- Page 2 comprehension → language noticing → controlled practice → discussion/writing.

Suggested density: light on Page 1, medium on Page 2.

## 4. Interview/profile spread

Best for family businesses, jobs, childhood memories, routines.

Use portraits, speaker labels, pull quotes and fact lines. Keep the main interview visually unified rather than dividing every answer into separate cards.

## 5. Quiz/questionnaire spread

Best for habits, roommate compatibility, shopping behavior, food preferences, healthy routines.

Use a strong poster/quiz field inside one exercise. Questions may form internal 2–3 column grids.

## 6. Real-world interface spread

Best for maps/reviews, transport apps, tickets, Lost and Found forms, online shopping, phone language.

Simulated interface surfaces may use more rounding and subtle shadows because the content is explicitly interface-like. Do not mimic proprietary screenshots unnecessarily.

## 7. Process/timeline spread

Best for directions, routines, recipes, travel steps, booking, food-sharing processes.

Use horizontal process strips, maps, timelines and sequencers inside individual exercises.

## 8. Collage/editorial spread

Best for fashion, travel, food culture, nostalgia and city-life comparisons.

Use one dominant image plus meaningful detail crops, captions or pull quotes. Avoid decorative collage pieces with no pedagogical role.

## Spread contrast

A lesson should normally have contrast between its two pages. Avoid two pages with identical density and identical composition.

Useful pairings:

- photo-led → practice-led
- reading-feature → language/practice
- quiz → speaking/writing
- real-world interface → language/communication
- collage → reading/comprehension

## Density scale

### Light

Large visuals or reading feature; typically 2–3 substantial exercises.

### Medium

Default lesson density; approximately 3–5 exercises with moderate internal layouts.

### Dense

Review, grammar-heavy or listening-heavy page with many short prompts. Use sparingly and maintain readable type.

CSS classes:

- `.hz-density-light`
- `.hz-density-medium`
- `.hz-density-dense`

## Controlled irregularity

A layout may occasionally break the normal frame by:

- extending a photograph toward a page edge;
- overlapping a caption or quote with a photograph;
- using a slightly rotated photographic insert;
- extending a large structural shape beyond its containing text block.

Do not rotate or overlap answer lines, exercise numerals, grammar tables or other interaction mechanics.

## Unit tendencies

These are tendencies, not mandatory templates:

- Unit 1: clean identity/introduction layouts
- Unit 2: object crops, labels, possessions, family/business portraits
- Unit 3: timelines, routines, maps/reviews
- Unit 4: food photography, ingredient/process layouts
- Unit 5: fashion/editorial crops and shopping interfaces
- Unit 6: plans, maps, spatial diagrams and route layouts
- Unit 7: diary/archival/photo-memory treatments
- Unit 8: travel-poster, route and weather/news compositions

The syllabus remains authoritative. Visual tendency must never alter lesson content or objectives.
