## 1. Grammar

Example 0: works.
1 gets; 2 don't; 3 Does; 4 do; 5 am always; 6 us; 7 them; 8 milk; 9 an egg.

The items cover present-simple third-person affirmative, negative and question forms; frequency-adverb position with be; object pronouns for plural subjects and objects; and the food/drink senses of uncountable milk and countable egg. Do not generalize the milk item to ordering portions of drinks in contexts not taught here.

## 2. Vocabulary

Example 0: Friday (not a drink).
1 noisy (not a day); 2 sour (not a cooking action); 3 milk (not an adjective); 4 rice (not a countable food noun in the taught food sense).

The category labels make the criterion explicit. Do not ask learners to guess an arbitrary semantic difference among the foods.

## 3. Food for four

The requirement is four apples, four bananas and four eggs. The photo shows A exactly two apples; B exactly six individual bananas; C exactly four eggs.
A not enough; B too many; C enough; D too much (three bottles of milk for a one-bottle requirement).

The instruction requires all four bank entries, one per response. Enough means sufficient, including more than the minimum; it does not mean exactly four. Six bananas are also enough in everyday language, but the four-entry matching task assigns too many to the surplus countable group, too much to surplus milk and enough to the exact food requirement. Explain this if a learner questions B; do not teach an incorrect exact-equality definition of enough.

The illustration is generated product photography, not a real meal or food-safety guide. It is a planning inventory; no learner is asked to eat uncooked eggs. Food counts and spacing were checked against the actual image, not merely its prompt.

The six statements have a 3/3 yes/no balance and direct printed evidence. The last item tests before/after using the explicitly stated sequence. ## 6. Your cinema night

Answers vary. Check that the message communicates a day, a time, a place and food. It must contain a meaningful because or so relationship; do not require both or impose fixed sentence stems.

Possible model:

Hi! I don't work on Friday, so I go to the park at six. The film is at seven. I have apples and sandwiches. I don't have meat because I don't eat it. Do you want a sandwich?

Accept other sensible plans and food choices. Check present-simple forms, pronoun reference, countable/uncountable food language and vocabulary only where the learner uses them. Punctuation: capital I, names/days and sentence starts; full stops/question marks; a comma before so between these complete clauses. Preserve apostrophes in I'm and don't. Eight 7 mm open lines support independent expression. Do not make this another food-sharing-app post or a fill-in-the-gaps writing task.

## 7. BEYOND HORIZONS

Choose one, change roles and verify the product. Learners use their own or fictional information, not a facing visible answer table.

| Choice | Evidence to check |
| --- | --- |
| A day in order | Three comprehensible present-simple routines, usually and one sensible before/after relationship. Partner asks a question and identifies which event is first. |
| Food for friends | A stated food requirement and private available amounts for four people. Enough/not enough match those amounts; partner checks and suggests a practical purchase/change. No actual food purchase or eating is required. |
| A place we like | A place, when the speaker goes, who accompanies them and a reason for liking it. Partner asks about a detail and reports one new fact. Accept fictional places/details. |

Use the notes space for the chosen task. At the end, improve one answer or adapt a useful example using the relevant source lesson. This retry creates observable language evidence, not a decorative self-rating.
