# Horizons — expressive visual identity

The author's own coursebook is the primary reference for the emotional character of this series. English File, Personal Best and Evolve remain useful references for editorial craft. The author's correction of 31 October 2026 supersedes the assumption that adult professionalism requires quiet colors, square forms or neutralized learning graphics.

## The character to preserve

Horizons should feel warm, colourful, curious and recognisably made by an imaginative teacher. It can be witty and playful while treating adult learners with respect. Colourful number bubbles, lively letterforms, visible graphic relationships, purposeful cutouts and tactile documents belong to this identity. Do not remove them as generic cleanup.

The personal reference makes concepts memorable through physical metaphors, chromatic groupings, shaped tabs, photographs that interact with the page and occasional unexpected visual details. Transfer those principles with original Horizons content. Do not impose the reference's dog mascot, juvenile clip art, crowded ornament or advanced B2 language on A1.

## What stays consistent across books

- The crimson navigation family, legible body typography, clear exercise numbers, recurring programme identities and recognizable functional labels form the series backbone.
- Grammar, vocabulary and communication tools have readable functional accents. Local concept diagrams can give different roles distinct colours, with explicit words or shapes so colour is never the only clue.
- Source features and learner artifacts have their own suitable visual worlds. Their title lettering, tabs, diagrams, photographic relationships and colour can differ from the navigation palette.
- Body type, directions and ordinary models remain consistent. Alphabet letters, numbers, calendar dates and other visual learning objects may be larger and more expressive in the same appropriate family. These are intentional teaching graphics, not accidental font-size drift.
- White reading and writing areas, sufficient contrast and generous space protect usability. Saturated accents can coexist with those quiet areas.

## How to apply it

The author's further direction of 1 November 2026 rejects a pass that stops at colour accents and small ornaments. Reading features and concept explanations should have substantial original visual settings and memorable physical forms where they strengthen the lesson. Use imagegen actively for those designed stages: material environments, integrated props, bold surfaces and concept objects with a noticeable presence on the spread. A narrow paper rim or tiny corner object alone does not fulfil this brief. Keep ordinary reading copy live, legible and genuinely supported by the visual concept.

Vary the integration as well as the object. Speech pods can hold parallel examples; an interview console can carry portraits with longer source columns below; a diary can hold a continuous narrative; an exhibition or cinema structure can stage a title and photograph while the reading stays on open paper. Avoid giving every lesson the same tall frame, photo-and-paragraph stack or empty mock document. A substantial visual idea is not permission to crowd every activity or add a new decorative character.

Give each new spread an explicit visual idea tied to the content. Record which expressive elements should survive future revisions. A calendar can show dates through stamps and a timeline; a number can live in a generous coloured bubble; an exchange can connect two distinct voices; a travel record can behave like a real document. Use the device that helps this lesson, not the device most recently used.

Choose local colour intentionally. There is no mandatory blue/green background plus yellow mark, no requirement to recolour every object to crimson and no rainbow quota. Avoid a sequence of nearly identical muted features. Review adjacent spreads together for visual fatigue and contrasting memorable details.

Retain approved photos, cutouts and generated surfaces when they work. Use imagegen freely for original task-serving objects, specific poses, scenes or material details; keep learner words and writing lines live. Simple circles, labels, diagrams and semantic colours belong in editable HTML/CSS/SVG. Do not generate decorative characters just to make a page seem creative.

## Review before release

1. Does the spread have a memorable content-related visual idea and an inviting emotional tone?
2. Have approved expressive details survived? Compare the previous edition, especially number/letter graphics, diagrams, source headings and distinctive local shapes.
3. Does colour help recognition, grouping, contrast or personality without revealing an answer or carrying meaning alone?
4. Is the playfulness appropriate for the intended learners? Look for adult subject matter, clear craft and restraint in the number of competing details.
5. Are the tasks, source facts, recording words, crops, reading order and writing space still sound?
6. Does the saved PDF preserve the effect, and does the published viewer agree?

The audit must record a real visual judgment and protected elements per spread. Inventory and a geometry pass cannot substitute for it. A visual cleanup that makes the book colder or more generic fails this standard even when it fits technically.
