# Concept readability revision

The author requested the corrected 4A approach across the other lessons. All
numbered activities, source readings/dialogues and recordings retain their
wording and answer information. These notes describe revised clarification
models; the activity keys remain applicable.

- **1A:** affirmative, negative and question models share the teacher context;
  am/is/are and negative not are selectively emphasized.
- **1B:** a/an examples identify the consonant/vowel SOUND distinction. The
  full-to-contracted flows retain corresponding complete sentences. The
  pronunciation table pairs written u in busy/fun with /ɪ/ and /ʌ/, removing
  the competing incorrect pronunciation strings. No new audio is required.
- **1D:** capital initials stand out in names, places, sentence openings and I.
  The email example is copied as provided; it is not a rule that email
  addresses can never contain capital letters. The canonical category is
  SKILLS, with the familiar label Capital letters.
- **2A:** existing pictures are grouped by near/far, then singular/plural.
  The labels explain the relation without requiring near/far as productive
  vocabulary. Headphones retains its plural form even for one headset.
- **2B:** a/an introduces one bus/app; the identifies the specific bus/app.
  The named Moovit example is a language model, not a new listening answer.
  The original plate example remains present.
- **2C:** only the possessive mark is emphasized: Diego's, Carlos's and
  parents'. Retain the book's chosen Carlos's form and the contrast between a
  singular name ending in s and a plural noun ending in s.
- **2D:** English and Spanish examples align by row. Noun underlining links
  phone/teléfono; adjective emphasis shows its different position. The English
  rule is before the item and after is, not adjective order. The Spanish model
  illustrates this ordinary color-adjective example, not an absolute rule for
  every Spanish adjective.
- **3A:** the same verbs and complements align across I/she. Emphasize s/es
  and the irregular has; do not present has as have plus an ending.
- **3B:** each negative model, question and matching short-answer pair is
  visibly aligned. Underlined work remains the base form with do/does and
  don't/doesn't. These illustrative yes/no options do not alter the guests'
  recorded schedules or the controlled-practice answers.
- **3C:** question words align with their answer information: place, companion,
  day and time. Who ... with asks for the companion. The auxiliary and main
  verb do not compete with the question word for emphasis.
- **3D:** the frequency continuum is qualitative; equal positions are not
  fixed numeric thresholds. Main verbs and be are underlined to locate the
  adverb. The temporal flows show walk preceding dinner and breakfast
  preceding water; both noun phrases and finite clauses remain modeled.

The adjective reading/categorization in **1C**, the paired-unit **Review**,
alphabet and number displays, and the corrected **4A** comparison were audited
and retained. No unnecessary concept box is added to those tasks. Existing
