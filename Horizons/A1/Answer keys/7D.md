# 7D — Dear diary

Writing D: an original diary entry, supported by seven irregular past verbs and sequencers. Printed pages 65–66. The historical hook is short; the lesson culminates in independent writing rather than another reading-led B lesson.

1. Yes. Pepys's diary included small everyday things. The cheese-in-the-garden example is based on his Great Fire account. The lesson is an original simplified paraphrase, not a direct quotation or a claim that the portrait shows a diary: the actual 1666 John Hayls portrait includes a music sheet. Museum source: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/samuel-pepys/ ; corroboration https://www.mola.org.uk/discoveries/news/chalk-and-cheese-samuel-pepys-ten-trinity-square and https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/samuel-pepys-will/ . Checked 28 October 2026. Pepys buried cheese during the 1666 Great Fire, specifically September 4; the pupil text states the year and context without assessing the exact day. Under the ground explains burial without a hidden new verb.
2. Track 7.10: get → got; go → went; have → had; see → saw; eat → ate; buy → bought; write → wrote. Jessica models all pairs. Learners are not expected to infer these irregular forms from the -ed rule.
3. Track 7.11: Tessa's shoes. One black, one brown, explicitly in both original text and commissioned image. Tessa is Amy, track label Jessica. Fictional diary. Monday, September 14th is valid in 2026; no diary year/age is assessed. 5. Track 7.12: in printed order 4, 1, 3, 2. Audio chronology: got up at eight → had breakfast → went to market and bought bread → went home and wrote in diary. This is a separate practice day, not contradictory details about Tessa's earlier bank day.
6. Private notes: where, who, one memorable thing. Full 6mm note lines, real writing photo, no pre-filled prescribed story. Students choose an ordinary small event rather than need an expensive trip or dramatic experience.
7. Open diary entry with date, title and ordered events. Eight full 7mm writing lines, not restrictive gaps. Check intelligibility, past verbs and logical sequencing; First/Then/After that/Finally are useful supports, not an obligation to force exactly four events. Partner reads and asks one real question, rather than guessing a visible answer. A fictional day can replace sensitive personal disclosure.

Focus: date and full sequence examples; went/saw; wrong/right I didn't went → I didn't go. Use past be and did/didn't from 7A/7C; irregular verbs do not change the auxiliary rule.
