# Horizons A1 - 5D: At the store

Student's Book pages 47-48. D speaking lesson: obtain groceries through availability, price and payment questions, then record a completed classroom transaction. The checkout-free comparison supports asking for help; it does not turn D into a second reading/listening lesson.

1. 1 receipt F; 2 cashier A; 3 card E; 4 customer B; 5 cash D; 6 checkout C. A is the worker holding the payment reader on the left; B is the customer with the basket on the right; C marks the checkout equipment/counter. D/E/F are separate object cutouts. Track 5.10 models all six words in the printed order.
2. Bread and milk. Receipt total: $5. Payment: card. The bread/milk prices are invented classroom prices shown on the receipt; the spoken total is five dollars. The customer asks for eggs, but the cashier says the store does not have any. No eggs belong on the receipt. This is a speaking model with supporting audio, not an independent listening test whose answers must be hidden from the printed model.
3. 1 b; 2 d; 3 a; 4 c. Underline please in Yes, please and I'd like some bread and some milk, please. The model shows the functions in context before the skills clarification. Thank you is another polite expression to notice, although the explicit search asks for please.
4. Online. The voice model asks how to pay and how to get a receipt. The illustrated fictional store uses an entry-card setup; it is not an image of a real Amazon location. Accept any reasoned preference for a conventional or cashier-free store in the paired follow-up. I like this store because... recycles the previous unit's because. No viewpoint about automation, job replacement, technology access or buying behavior is required.
5. Private preparation on separate paper. A chooses three foods. B independently chooses four of the six foods, assigns each a whole-dollar price from $1 to $5, and chooses cash, card or both. Keep these actual choices out of the partner's view. Both learners can read the printed instructions and word bank; those do not disclose each other's decisions. Stock and prices are not prefilled on a common visible answer card.
6. Open transaction. A asks about stock, chooses three available foods, asks about prices and payment, and records the foods/prices, payment method and total. If an item is unavailable, choose another from the offered stock; the model's unavailable eggs give the precedent. B has four foods, so three purchases are possible. Switch roles and make new private choices; a second record can be made on separate paper. Whole-dollar addition serves the practical purchase rather than becoming an abstract calculation exercise. Accept natural short replies and respectful alternative wording. Check successful information exchange and a concrete purchase record, not memorization of every model line.

Extra Practice: collect three grocery prices online or in a store and use them in a new spoken conversation. No purchase is required. A family member/classmate may supply prices when a website or store visit is unavailable.

## Functional language and cumulative ceiling

Explicit new vocabulary: cashier, customer, checkout, cash, card, receipt. Explicit functional frames: Do you have any...?, I'd like some..., please; How much is it?; Can I pay by card?; Excuse me. How do I pay?; Anything else? and appropriate short responses. Any/some and Can I...? are taught here as useful shopping chunks, not unannounced new quantifier rules or the Unit 6 can/can't ability syllabus. Food, prices, buy/have/pay/choose, questions with do, work and polite classroom exchanges recycle earlier material. The skill is speaking; the brief models, vocabulary recording, matching and skills box prepare it.

The technology input uses a short current description, not a completed purchase narrative forced into present simple. Automatically is a context-supported cognate. Leave, technology and online receipt are limited to the supported real-world comparison/help model; no app registration, sensor vocabulary, banking details or advanced procedural English is assessed. The model can be performed slowly with natural turn-taking, then the roles must use unknown information rather than reproduce printed answers.
