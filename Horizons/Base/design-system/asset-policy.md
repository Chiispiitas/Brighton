## General medium principles for future authorized image work

The author explicitly requests generous use of imagegen for redesigned sections, document surfaces, boxes and purposeful decorations. Use it actively where it gives a feature its own material character: postcards, tags, notices, studio pads, sleeve notes, itineraries, journals and tickets. Do not interpret the rejection of drawn portrait substitutions as a ban on generated design assets. Keep the learner copy, answers, labels and writing rules live over these surfaces, with white quiet text areas and safe inner margins. Preserve native alpha and original output bytes; inspect the actual finished page, not just the isolated asset. Avoid making a new universal paper-card template or enclosing every activity.

- Start from the teaching job and the necessary visible evidence.
- Prefer authentic, well-composed photographs for ordinary people, real places and social situations. Keep natural context and useful imperfections; avoid anonymous polished pseudo-stock faces.
- For exact body gestures, quantities, routes and fictional action sequences, use clear original illustration or a deterministic native diagram when it communicates better. Keep an intentional style across a connected set. An illustration should look drawn; it need not imitate a camera.
- Use built-in imagegen when a new bitmap teaching asset materially helps. Keep original output bytes, versioned filenames and native alpha for transparent assets. Never substitute code-drawn raster lookalikes for a requested photograph or illustration. Simple maps, arrows, labels, charts and editable documents belong in live HTML/SVG where appropriate.
- Photo-real generation requires an explicit reason and print-scale scrutiny. Reject waxy skin, repeated faces, anatomical/tool/action errors, incoherent lighting, text artifacts, synthetic empty rooms and unnecessary cinematic polish. Do not keep an asset simply because it took time to generate.
- Do not remove high-quality existing cutouts solely because they were generated. Keep them when their teaching role, anatomy, style and integration are strong. Replace weak ones with a better medium rather than trying to conceal their origin.

## Inspection and provenance

Visual spirit is governed by VISUAL-SPIRIT.md. Preserve the author's approved expressive objects, local colour relationships and purposeful cutouts during media cleanup. The personal B2 reference supplies emotional and conceptual principles; its mascot and third-party photographs are not automatically new Horizons assets. New physical metaphors should be original and useful to the current learning task.

View every final asset and its actual crop at print size. Preserve whole faces and answer-bearing hands/actions/counts/objects. Judge the opaque subject rather than the canvas dimensions; keep alpha edges clean. Repeated picture tasks need fresh visual evidence, not a slightly different crop of the source photo. A picture should not disclose a private task answer.

Record file, creator/generator, original source URL, license URL, dimensions, hash, changes, actual active page uses and fiction/documentary boundaries. Generated illustrations need the final prompt set and actual tool; real models must not be misrepresented as the named fictional speaker. Exact historic/event claims need documentary evidence; a weather photograph may illustrate conditions without documenting the named event.

Preserve old media files; mark superseded uses inactive. New end credits list active external photographs with required attribution and license/change notices. Keep detailed provenance in manifests; no licensing strips in learner tasks. Reference coursebook pages inspire principles and are never copied into the product.
