# Grammar practice — lessons 5A–8D

Numbers restart within each main lesson. The original controlled answers and open-response criteria are retained; page references and activity numbers follow the lesson-specific practice.

## 5A — What are you wearing? · page 92

**1.** 1 is; 2 am; 3 are; 4 Is.

**2.** 1 She is wearing new shoes. 2 They aren’t talking. 3 Is he reading? Yes, he is.

**3.** 1 wear; am wearing. 2 works; is eating. Contractions acceptable.

**4.** Open: one affirmative and one negative present-continuous sentence about current actions. Check subject + am/is/are (+ not) + -ing. Accept invented details if observing classmates would be intrusive.

## 5B — That’s my favorite! · page 93

**1.** 1 too; 2 enough; 3 enough; 4 too.

**2.** 1 This bag is big enough. 2 These shoes aren’t comfortable enough.

## 5C — How often do you shop? · page 94

**1.** 1 Twice a week. 2 Once a week.

**2.** every week; twice a week; every day.

## 6B — My dream home · page 95

**1.** The six diagram labels are, in reading order: in; on; under; next to; above; in front of. Learners cover the labels for oral retrieval, and the partner checks after each response. No unintroduced synonyms such as above/higher than are required.

**2.** 1 The phone is on the table. 2 The bag is under the table. 3 The chair is next to the sofa. 4 The shoes are in the box.

**3.** Open. Learners draw a room with three objects on separate paper; both partners use this shared scene. The speaker chooses one object privately, without naming it, writes/says two accurate location sentences, and the partner points to the intended object. Accept any two taught prepositions with clear relations in the actual drawing. The partner’s identification checks that the description works. The teaching diagram models in front of by overlapping the red ball in front of the box; under uses a table top and legs.

## 6C — My neighborhood · page 96

**1.** 1 straight; 2 right; right.

**2.** left; left. The start arrow faces north/up; directions use the traveler’s perspective, not the page reader’s changing perspective.

## 7A — When I was little… · page 98

## 7B — I was born in July! · page 99

**1.** 1 in; 2 on; 3 in.

**2.** 1 one; 2 ago. The explicit example date is August 10th, not a claim about the classroom date.

## 7C — Life in the 80s · page 100

**1.** visited; worked; lived; studied; planned; played.

**2.** 1 played; 2 danced; 3 stayed; 4 didn’t dance.

**3.** 1 I studied on Sunday. 2 We didn’t play music. 3 Did she visit a friend?

**4.** Open. Two different past-simple questions beginning Did + subject + base verb; truthful short answers from the partner. There is a real information gap because the partner’s weekend is unknown. Check did/didn’t in the short answer. Four normal writing lines provide a question and answer per exchange.

## 8A — Let’s go to Toronto! · page 101

**1.** 1 arrived; 2 left; 3 took; 4 ate.

**2.** 1 She traveled by train last week. 2 We stayed for two nights. 3 They visited the market yesterday. 4 I left my bag at the hotel. The book follows American traveled; accept travelled if consistently taught locally.

**3.** 1 Did you take photos? 2 Did she leave her bag at the hotel? 3 Did they travel by train?

**4.** Open. Two affirmative past-simple sentences and one negative sentence with didn’t + base verb. Real or imagined trips are valid. Do not require disclosure of travel history.

## 8B — It’s so cold! · page 102

**1.** 1 c; 2 a; 3 b.

**2.** 1 Should I take a coat? 2 You shouldn’t go outside. Affirmative base-verb form is already modeled and retrieved in task1; these two repairs focus on the question and negative.

## 8C — Can you go tomorrow? · page 103

**1.** 1 has; 2 hurts; 3 have; 4 hurt.

**2.** 1 I have a cold. 2 My leg hurts. Open message: one understandable invented complaint after because, plus a suggested day. For example, I can’t come today because I have a cold. Can we meet on Sunday? No medical diagnosis or personalized treatment is being assessed.
