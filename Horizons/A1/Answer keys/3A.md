# Horizons A1 - Lesson 3A: What's your routine?

Student's Book pages 23-24. Master: `Lessons/3A.html`.
Unit 3: My Life. Focus: daily-routine vocabulary and affirmative present simple.

## Answers

1. Example 1 B have breakfast; 2 H go to bed; 3 C go to work; 4 A get up; 5 G watch TV; 6 D have lunch; 7 E go home; 8 F have dinner.
2. Prediction: **one**. Students guess before reading/listening, then check the source; an initial wrong prediction is not marked as a language error.
3. 1 seven / 7:00; 2 one / 1:00; 3 Antonela; 4 school.
4. 1 a routine; 2 gets; 3 watch.
5. 1 I get up at 6:30. 2 Mia goes to work at 8:00. Accept times written in words and correct final punctuation.
6. Nina: get up 6:30 (example), go to work 8:00, go home 6:00, go to bed 10:00. Omar: get up 10:00, go to work 2:00, go home 11:00, go to bed 12:30. The spoken routines establish the order; AM/PM notation is optional and not assessed.
7. Four accurate first-person affirmative sentences about the learner, with no name. Use familiar routines and times. The printed model is not one of the four response lines. Learners write the same text on an unsigned loose slip for the following exchange; the book lines provide their draft. A folded/covered page may be used if loose paper is unavailable.
8. Personal answers. Collect/mix the unsigned texts within groups and give each learner someone else's text. Keep authors private until guesses are made. Learners read and identify an author with Is it ...?, then confirm with the writer and report orally using third-person sentences. The new task avoids duplicating the four-sentence writing activity. The guess is not a graded deduction: use routines familiar to the group, accept uncertainty and let the writer reveal their identity. Check get/gets, go/goes, have/has and watch/watches. Do not assess handwriting recognition. This supplies a genuine unknown author; students must not just read their own signed exercise to a partner.

Extra Practice: choose a person in your family and write five affirmative sentences about their routine. Check third-person verb endings and the taught phrases. A fictional family member is also acceptable.

## Teaching sequence and language ceiling

Activity 1 establishes eight pictured phrases. Activities 2-3 put them into a real routine; activity 4 directs noticing of habitual meaning and singular/plural forms. The shared grammar focus clarifies I/you/we/they versus he/she/it, including go/goes, watch/watches and have/has. Activity 5 retains two contrasting controlled sentences. Activity 6 contrasts teacher/chef schedules in two larger photo-and-time records and asks for a personal comparison. Activities 7-8 turn personal routine writing into an anonymous author-guessing task and third-person reporting. In activity 1, the teacher can briefly hide the word bank after matching for oral recall; demonstrate rather than add unfamiliar learner instructions.

Do not introduce do/does questions or don't/doesn't in this lesson: those are 3B targets. The authorized syllabus title is retained as an established prompt. Do not introduce frequency adverbs or before/after connectors from 3D. Do not require present continuous, past forms or a full food syllabus. The date in the feature identifies the source context; learners are not asked to produce historical narrative.

The eight daily phrases and affirmative forms are the target language. The short club/training context explains Messi's work; `club`, `routine`, `special`, `Argentina`, `Miami` and `TV` are transparent or established, and `trains` has the pictured athlete and work context. `school`, family, children, work, home, personal names and telling time build on preceding lessons. Source/license credits are publication metadata, not assessed learner vocabulary.

## Real-person evidence and source boundaries

The reading is an original A1 adaptation of Lionel Messi's account of his family-and-training routine in his September 2023 OLGA interview with Migue Granados. It describes that source context, not an independently verified current 2026 schedule. No bedtime, dinner time, dietary quantities or invented training timetable is attributed to him. Going to work paraphrases going to club training. His reported return home around 1 p.m. is simplified to one; no other exact celebrity times are invented.
