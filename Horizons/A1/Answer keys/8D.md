# 8D — Tickets, please!

Communication D, printed pages 73–74: telephone requests, one-way/return tickets, checking names/times/prices and a genuinely private booking exchange. City Tickets is a fictional classroom desk; every flight time, price and passenger record is invented practice data, not an airline schedule or offer.


1. Nora wants to visit her brother in Toronto; he is free today. The real photograph is an illustrative traveler, not a documentary portrait or actual customer's story. Same-day motivation is visible and modest; no unsupported biography.

2. Track 8.10: I'd like a ticket to Toronto, please; One way or return?; What time does it leave?; How much is it?; Can you repeat that?; Can you spell your name? All six phrases modeled by Jessica. I'd like is a functional polite chunk here, not a new conditional-tense syllabus.

3. Track 8.11: $120. Nora books one way to Toronto today. Departure 3:15, arrival 4:20, surname Lee spelled L-E-E. Agent Mark Natural, Nora Adeline, label Jessica. Printed transcript matches the recording. Arrival and departure remain distinct.

4. 1 say it again; 2 a one-way; 3 3:15. The source provides all answers; repeat is distinct from asking for spelling.

5. Track 8.12, independent call: To Ottawa; Leaves 10:30; Price $90. Return and Ben Fox are given. Surname is spelled F-O-X. Agent Juniper, traveler Mark Casual. These are fresh call details, not the answers from Nora's visible transcript. Equivalent $90/ninety dollars and 10:30/ten thirty are normalized without changing their values.

6. Prepare private roles: traveler chooses destination/day, morning or afternoon, ticket type and how much they can pay; agent prepares one morning and one afternoon flight with prices. Keep notes hidden. Ask about alternatives if an offer is too expensive.

7. Negotiate a suitable ticket and check passenger/destination/day/time/price/ticket type. A justified no-ticket decision is also successful communication: write why no offer works. Change roles. The budget and offers are unknown beforehand, not facing printed answers.


Language support: Polite ticket requests, one-way/return, time/price, repeat and spell; add price negotiation with familiar too expensive. Recorded bookings are fictional practice data, not actual offers.
