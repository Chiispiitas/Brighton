# 6C - My neighborhood

Language C: prepositions of movement and giving directions. Printed pages 53-54. Context -> visual noticing/matching -> model conversation -> guided noticing -> explicit clarification -> controlled route listening/choices -> genuine local route exchange -> transfer. Can/cannot and past forms are not required.

1. Personal answers. The stock photo shows two adults walking along a city sidewalk; it is not evidence of a numeric Walk Score, a safety rating or a fictional person's identity. Walk Score is a real 0-100 measure for an address, using distance to amenities and pedestrian-friendly road/density measures. The short original paraphrase is supported by https://www.walkscore.com/methodology.shtml (checked 26 October 2026). More nearby amenities help the score; the printed introduction does not claim this is the only factor. It does not assign a fake official score to the pictured street or the practice map. On foot means walking. The final 1-5 rating is each student's subjective judgment of a particular walk, not an official Walk Score conversion.
2. Track 6.7: 1 B (across); 2 A (along); 3 D (past); 4 C (through). Across crosses from one side to the other; along follows the street/sidewalk; through enters and passes inside the park; past continues beyond the bank. The bank is a generic conventional public-building illustration, not a real bank. The selective flat ELT artwork is justified by direction clarity; it does not replace the lesson's real street photograph or photographic model map. Arrows supply travel evidence; do not infer the movement from a static person's facing direction alone.
3. Track 6.8: No. It is five minutes on foot. Two unnamed fictional adults ask about a supermarket. The directions agree with the continuation map: face up from START, reach the park at the main intersection, turn to the traveler's right, pass the bank, and the supermarket is on the traveler's left. The five-minute distance belongs to this invented conversation; it is not a real measured route.
4. Go straight; go past the bank. Right follows turn in the source. Oral noticing: no added answer lines. Left is clarified by its paired functional arrow in the focus box. Start a direction with the base verb: Go, Turn, not Goes/Turns. The four movement models and right/left arrows distinguish meaning at ordinary 9.5pt. Do not substitute a metalanguage puzzle or shrink labels.
5. Track 6.9: 1 library; 2 supermarket; 3 café. Each route restarts from the upward START arrow, not from the preceding destination. Left/right refer to the traveler's direction of travel. First route turns west: library is south/left. Second route turns east, passes the north-side bank, then reaches the north-side supermarket on the left. Third route turns east: café is south/right, next to the museum. The outer lower road is background model geography; START is already on the north-facing connecting street. No distance scale, crossing-safety decision or real trip is assessed. All six destinations are labeled live, with no generated sign text.
6. 1 left; 2 past; 3 right; 4 across. The last item explicitly changes the starting place to the bank: the café is directly across the horizontal street. Do not interpret that item as beginning at START. Across/along are meaningful contrasts, not unexplained abstract formulas.
7. Personal route. The destination comes from the learner's own knowledge and remains unstated until guessed, so a shared page does not reveal the answer. Two open 6mm note lines support planning without prescribing the final language. An imagined nearby place is acceptable if local knowledge is unavailable. Students give a personal 1-5 judgment of the walk with a simple reason (e.g., The park is near / I like the trees / There are many cars may be teacher-supported but is not required). Prefer taught be/have/simple present: The street has many cars. No invented route should be used as real-world navigation.

Extra Practice: independently write a known walking route from home to a place, then ask someone familiar with it to check the directions. No public posting, private address disclosure or mandatory off-site class walk is required.

Support-language ceiling: prior be/have/do, present simple/continuous, questions, near/far, places, numbers/time; explicit movement set and straight/left/right/go/turn. On foot is glossed, walk/neighborhood are contextual. Address/services/points relate to the transparent score artifact; do not add a technical walkability reading load. Modal can remains 6D's new rule. Teacher notes may use more advanced explanatory language than the learner pages.
