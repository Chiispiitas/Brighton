# Horizons — practice-bank standard

Practice banks consolidate the language actually taught in the unit. Use the current lesson masters and wordlists, not an older syllabus outline. Every lesson reference must resolve to a substantive topic, its actual printed page and a working link in standalone and assembled views. A short visible lesson code returns to the source lesson. Keep front matter, workbook and optional annex scope separate.

Grammar needs a clear teaching route: visible meaning, a short accurate form contrast, focused practice and useful independent use. Use counted objects, spatial diagrams, timelines, reference arrows, aligned clauses or another concept-specific visual when it clarifies the target. Words remain live and easy to read. Ordinary concepts and task text use 10pt, instructions 9.2pt; functional bank/topic headings and navigation have consistent roles. Mark the exact changing element selectively. Do not substitute a dense table, an attractive empty box or a repeated physical stage for explanation.

For tenses and other finite-verb patterns, use a compact Positive / Negative / Yes-no question chart with parallel complete examples. Show changes through short labels, insertion or swap arrows, aligned word groups and useful short-answer models. Be and modal verbs move before the subject in yes/no questions; ordinary present/past verbs require do/does/did plus the base verb. These distinctions must remain visually explicit. Learner explanation areas use diagrams, charts and short labels, not inline rule sentences or procedural paragraphs. Full-sentence language models, contextual facts needed for a task and task instructions remain appropriate. Avoid several overlapping charts explaining the same thing. Non-tense concepts retain a picture, scale, spatial diagram or concise mapping when clearer. Preserve 10pt teaching type and usable writing space, removing a redundant drill before compressing the explanation. This final author clarification supersedes the earlier plan to add verbal procedural rules.

The author endorsed the current fresh-game vocabulary activities on 1 November 2026 and then clarified that each unit also needs a study word bank before its activities. Include the complete topic vocabulary and useful chunks from the actual lessons, with grouped meaning cues, examples or pictures as needed for revision before a test. Distinguish core targets, modest practice-only scaffolds and incidental names. Do not limit the reference to whichever words the games happen to test. Repeating the taught words is the purpose of the reference; copying the lesson's picture sheet is not required. Keep the approved games, original practice resources and reserved future Blooket areas.

Every vocabulary topic begins with a substantive word-bank reference, followed by a real link/page pointer to its activities. Activity headings return to that topic, and printed activity headers identify the unit's word-bank pages. Use a source-mapped lexical inventory so later edits cannot silently omit necessary words. Add reference pages before shrinking ordinary 10pt type or approved response space. Memory tasks explicitly require covering the word bank as well as their original hidden evidence. Additional print-only reference words or pictures do not imply a new audio recording.

Vocabulary input needs explicit visual evidence and retrieval. Use approved whole subjects, purposeful imagegen cutouts, photographed objects and scenes. Mix picture naming, memory, meaningful sorting, word chains, clue solving, repair and personal use. Check that a game is playable from the supplied information; do not promise that two clues always determine an answer. A memory task must say what to cover. Picture labels, times and visual pointers must make the controlled answer unambiguous. A private-choice task must establish what stays private. Give an independent option when no partner is available and it serves the same task.

The author's practice-art correction of 1 November 2026 supersedes the earlier native-illustration approach for grammar and vocabulary banks. All pictorial teaching assets in these banks use built-in imagegen: objects, food quantities, clocks, furniture, weather, maps, anatomical images and positional scenes. Do not substitute SVG clipart, flat geometric drawings, CSS-drawn phones/food/objects or schematic vector towns. Aim for real material detail, clear silhouettes, natural people, whole subjects and useful scale. Inspect exact counts, clock times, routes, positions and answer-bearing details; regenerate a wrong image rather than disguising it with code. Retain original generated files and native alpha, version edits, record prompts and actual uses.

Every vocabulary study page and activity page must contain at least one useful topic-specific image, including pages that previously relied entirely on word lists, cards or tables. A token heading icon, native table or crossword grid does not satisfy this requirement. Use a modest but legible cutout or a stronger photo source as the teaching job requires, without making every page the same. Keep reference and game resources distinct where memory/retrieval depends on the evidence. Native words, grammar form charts, tables, arrows, editorial callouts, answer grids and writing rules remain live, crisp and accessible; they are not pictorial substitutes. The user’s preferred visual grammar explanations continue to apply.

Do not repeat every lesson story or impose one activity template. Stable navigation and clear typography can coexist with different pictures, response mechanisms and local visual ideas. Vocabulary practice must use new practice-specific resources and games, rather than repeating the lesson picture sheets, diagrams or body photographs. Familiar words are retrieved in a fresh source, clue set, choice, puzzle or situation. Grammar may briefly repeat a taught model when it clarifies a new practice task. No universal palette or generated-feature quota applies. Keep adequate whitespace, contrast and response space. Ordinary open writing rules are at least 7mm, and the final empty rule must be fully inside the printable region. Test empty fields as well as visible text.

Keys cover every controlled activity and identify accepted alternatives and open-response success. Recorded models must match any claimed listening content; no fake audio labels. Additional practice vocabulary requires an explicit visible model and a wordlist note. Credit every reused component photograph at every active printed-page use, including all components of collages. Preserve originals and a reversible baseline.

Before publication, validate destination and return links, page order, image decoding, source/key alignment, actual ordinary fonts, every empty response field, standalone/assembled parity and the reopened saved PDF. Inspect the whole bank for reading rhythm and visual fatigue. Record independent content/visual judgments and the exact corrected proof. Counts and clean geometry alone do not establish teaching quality or author approval.

## Future Blooket spaces

Reserve one clearly identified QR area per unit in each of the grammar and vocabulary banks. Leave the QR square blank until a real game URL is supplied. Do not invent a game, fake a scannable pattern, print an inactive scan instruction or make a placeholder link. Register sixteen stable slots with null URLs/QR assets. The reserved area must stay clear of writing lines, pictures and instructions in the saved PDF; recompose locally without shrinking teaching type or writing-row height. The intended future uses are independent revision and teacher-led class play.
