# 1C — Where are you from?

Student's Book pages 9–10. Editorial edition, 31 October 2026.

1. Lionel Messi — Argentina; Taylor Swift — the US; Keanu Reeves — Canada; Millie B. Brown — the UK; Lee Jung-jae — South Korea. The celebrity photographs illustrate the people; country association here is the listening/coursebook nationality target and is not a question about every person's birthplace.
2. Canada — Canadian; the US — American; South Korea — South Korean; Argentina — Argentinian; the UK — British.
3–4. Personal city choice supported by the text. Example: Rio de Janeiro — big, beautiful, hot, friendly; Toronto — old/new buildings, cold, expensive food, famous restaurant; Hong Kong — busy city, calm parks, good/cheap food, beautiful at night, expensive hotels. Do not grade the preference itself.
5. Suggested sorting: Weather — hot, cold; Prices — expensive, cheap; Cities and places — big, beautiful, old, new, busy, calm, famous; People — friendly. Some adjectives fit more than one category in a sensible sentence. Good can describe food/places; do not impose a single exhaustive semantic taxonomy.
6. 1 big (beautiful is also supported); 2 friendly; 3 calm; 4 expensive; 5 old, new; 6 cold. The six item numbers now read in normal row order.
7. Secret-city information exchange. The learner chooses one source city privately; their partner asks up to three taught be questions and guesses. The printed Toronto model uses the source's cold weather and expensive food. Where the source says nothing about a property, the learner can say they do not know; do not invent required city facts.
8. Three independent sentences about the learner's city, followed by a partner question. The three restored city pictures are prompts/examples, not mandatory answers. An imagined city is acceptable. Check appropriate be forms, adjective position and capital letters, rather than requiring a template with every gap predetermined.

Extra Practice: ask a classmate about their city using the taught questions.

The unrecorded city posts use present descriptions rather than an unexplained future form. Today frames cold weather as an observation, not a permanent claim about a city. All existing recorded material and baseline artwork remain.
