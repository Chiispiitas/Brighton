# 3D - My week in order

Unit 3: My Life. A writing lesson on frequency adverbs and before/after, culminating in an original #MyWeek post. The six-routine introduction teaches walk, drink water, read a book, listen to music and sleep with explicit photographic support, and recycles have breakfast. Recycles 3A present simple, 3B days and negatives, earlier times, and 1D capitalization. No advice about water quantities, sleep targets, diet, weight or exercise intensity is taught.

## Answers

1. 1 F (drink water); 2 B (walk); 3 A (read a book); 4 C (listen to music); 5 D (have breakfast); 6 E (sleep).
2. Read/listen and compare the post with the week record. Water: seven days; walking: six; TV: two; bedtime at twelve: none. Saturday is the day Zoe does not walk. She watches TV on Tuesday and Saturday.
3. 1 yes; 2 no; 3 before; 4 after.
4. Photo letters in chronological order: **B, C, A**. B shows breakfast, C water after the finished breakfast, A leaving home with a work bag. The live timetable supplies the exact times and identifies the destination as work; the door photograph alone cannot prove a destination. Connector choices: 1 **after**; 2 **before**. Check the order and action meaning rather than reading left-to-right as chronology.
5. Personal planning. Choose three familiar routines, circle days and write a suitable adverb. Days left uncircled can represent never. Adverbs are qualitative, not fixed mathematical thresholds. A blank row is not itself a sentence and does not teach a false word order.
6. An independently worded #MyWeek post using planned adverbs and at least one before/after phrase. Four full writing lines plus a separate challenge line are provided. Choose one feasible familiar routine for a seven-day challenge, e.g. Read a book after dinner. The challenge line can use the familiar routine phrase or present-simple form; no future-tense construction is required. Partners compare posts; no social account or publication is needed.
Extra Practice: after seven days, write a fresh post and compare it with the first. Use present simple to describe the new typical week; past tense is not required. Students can find that nothing changed. Do not frame routines as medical advice or prescribe quantities/intensity. The goal is an actual follow-up to writing, not another unrelated sentence list.

## Teaching sequence

Introduce the five new action chunks and recycle have breakfast through six independent real photos and 3.8. Sleep describes being asleep; go to bed from 3A describes the preceding action. Keep that distinction when modeling personal sentences. The fictional challenge post models meaningful frequency and sequencing language, not a public influencer's claims. Its seven-day record gives visible evidence for always/usually/sometimes/never; the chosen six/two-day examples are illustrative, not fixed numerical definitions. Always means on every relevant occasion and never means on no relevant occasion; usually and sometimes describe flexible tendencies. Learners do not memorize percentage thresholds. The record is a model of one typical week; Zoe's be sentence is explicit text evidence for placement, not a fifth inferred diary row.

Locate the new adverbs in the post. Activity 3 connects frequency to the visible record and guides discovery of adverb position before a main verb and after be. The focus clarifies this distinction with live example sentences. For early A1, teach this default position; do not introduce exception lists, inversion, often/rarely, How often...? or a second frequency-expression syllabus.

The morning sequence precedes the before/after focus. Both noun phrases (after breakfast) and finite clauses (after I have breakfast) are modeled, using familiar routine verbs. Do not require after/before + -ing, future clauses or past narration. Activity 4 supplies a controlled photographic/timed sequence and two determinate connector choices. The redundant second block of before/after gaps has been removed. Activities 5-6 lead to a personal weekly plan, an independent post and a chosen routine challenge; the final peer comparison gives reading the posts a purpose. No actual social-media publication or account creation is required.
