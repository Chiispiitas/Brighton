## Meaning and progression

The grammar box aligns meaning, countable-plural forms and uncountable forms in one three-row table. It uses tomatoes and oil consistently across the examples, with one short word-order note and two aligned wrong-to-right examples below: cheese enough → enough cheese; tomatoes not enough → not enough tomatoes. The wrong phrases are visibly crossed out. These helper examples do not change the recipe, listening evidence or activity answers.

- Guided discovery: action photographs and short recipe → quantity conversation → noticing → explicit grammar comparison → form choice → photographic quantity decisions → collaborative ingredient planning.
- Countability recycles 4A; food opinions and object reference recycle 4B. Four common verbs keep the new vocabulary manageable: wash, cut, add, mix. Imperatives are presented as recipe chunks, not expanded into a separate imperative grammar syllabus.
- Too much / too many describe more than is wanted for the stated recipe. They do not label particular foods inherently unhealthy or establish universal portion sizes. Enough means a sufficient quantity, which can include a surplus; it does not mean exactly the target or prohibit having extra ingredients. The final model intentionally has four tomatoes when two are needed, yet answers “Do you have enough?” with yes. Having extra tomatoes at home is different from adding too many to the dish.
- Not enough means less than needed; the guide supplies the target recipe, and activity 5 contrasts one, five and two visible tomatoes against the two-tomato requirement. The phrase applies to both plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns. This lesson treats enough before nouns only; adjective + enough belongs to later material.
- The model quantities are classroom examples for a small side salad, expressed in ordinary spoonfuls. No precise serving, nutrition or medical claim is made. No cooking, food handling or tasting is required in class.
- Essential support: recipe, salad, need, spoon, oil, use and more; the recipe sheet, real photo and quantity examples supply their context. These are recorded in the wordlist. Keep the established instruction verbs. Do not add 4D because/so or future shopping/imperative extensions.
