# Horizons A1 - 5C: How often do you shop?

Student's Book pages 45-46. C language lesson: frequency expressions and four places in town, through genuine shopping and library habits.

1. 1 supermarket C; 2 library D; 3 market A; 4 clothes store B. Pictures establish distinct places, not fictional character identities.
2. At the library. Accept She goes to the library. This is a gist question before detailed retrieval; Mara says she does not buy books.
3. A: 1 twice a month; 2 once a year. B: 1 one time; 2 two times. Learners infer meaning from the source before consulting the grammar panel.
4. 1 once; 2 twice; 3 times; 4 week. Every takes a singular noun and no article. Once/twice/three times + a + singular period. How often do you / does she recycle earlier present-simple question forms.
5. 1 once a week; 2 twice a week; 3 every week; 4 three times a year. Accept once a week for 3 as an equivalent meaning; the recording says every week. The independent Noah recording supplies new information rather than repeating Mara's answers.
6. Open interview. Learners ask one another, record their partner's own answers and can use never. No partner answer sheet is visible or prefilled. Example exchange is a model, not a required answer. Frequency is personal; do not judge spending habits or access to a library.
7. Open report: two accurate sentences about the partner, including appropriate third-person form and frequency expressions. They then tell a different student who does not already know those answers. Encourage full independent sentences; the example is support rather than a gap template.

Extra Practice: ask someone in the family how often they shop and write three sentences. Another trusted person is acceptable when a family interview is unavailable. No purchase, personal financial disclosure or paid account is required.

## Language and genre

Mara's notes are an original fictional interview about regular habits. All verbs refer to actual routines, current preferences or a repeated pre-purchase question; they are not a completed transaction narrated incorrectly in present simple. Noah is an independent fictional listening speaker. Once a week at the supermarket and twice a month at the market describe different places, not contradictory total food-shopping counts. Every Saturday naturally clarifies Mara's supermarket routine. No fixed equation between weeks and months is taught.

Minimalist living is expressed accessibly through Do I need them?, infrequent clothes shopping and using the library instead of buying books. No definition of minimalism, unintroduced borrowing verb, budget calculation, value judgement or false sustainability claim is assessed. Food/clothes vocabulary, present-simple questions and third-person forms recycle previous units; the explicit new language is four place names and frequency expressions. This retains the C language role rather than replacing it with a reading lesson.
