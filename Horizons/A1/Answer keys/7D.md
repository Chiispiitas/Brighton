# 7D — Dear diary

Writing D: an original diary entry, supported by seven irregular past verbs and sequencers. Printed pages 65–66. The historical hook is short; the lesson culminates in independent writing rather than another reading-led B lesson.


1. Yes. Pepys's diary included small everyday things. The cheese-in-the-garden example is based on his Great Fire account. The lesson is an original simplified paraphrase, not a direct quotation or a claim that the portrait shows a diary: the actual 1666 John Hayls portrait includes a music sheet. 4. 1 go; 2 beginning; 3 end.

5. Track 7.12: in printed order 4, 1, 3, 2. Audio chronology: got up at eight → had breakfast → went to market and bought bread → went home and wrote in diary. This is a separate practice day, not contradictory details about Tessa's earlier bank day.

6. Private notes: where, who, one memorable thing. Full 6mm note lines, real writing photo, no pre-filled prescribed story. Students choose an ordinary small event rather than need an expensive trip or dramatic experience.

7. Write an open diary entry with date, title and ordered past events on eight full writing lines. A partner reads it, suggests an alternative title that reflects the meaning, and asks one real question. Judge intelligibility and logical sequencing, not a compulsory four-event formula. A fictional day is acceptable.


Language support: Date plus intelligible ordered events. Past be and did/didn’t rules still apply to irregular verbs. First, Then, After that and Finally are useful supports, not a fixed required story.


Extra Practice: Write another diary entry about a small thing worth remembering.
