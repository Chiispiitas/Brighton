# Horizons image direction

This guide controls photography and raster-image composition for the Horizons Student's Book.

## Core principle

Images are instructional and editorial assets, not decoration. A photograph should earn its space by establishing context, carrying vocabulary, supporting comprehension, or creating a memorable real-world feature.

## Composition rules

1. Prefer one strong hero image over several weak decorative images.
2. When a person appears beside text, prefer a crop in which the person's gaze/body direction leads toward the text rather than off the page.
3. Preserve usable negative space when a title, annotation, or reading overlay is planned.
4. Do not place essential faces, signs, objects, or vocabulary targets in the gutter-safe zone or beneath overlays.
5. Avoid center-cropping every photo. Use intentional left-, right-, top-, or detail-biased crops when the page composition benefits.
6. Inset images should show meaningful secondary information: an object, texture, sign, ingredient, accessory, map detail, room detail, or second moment.
7. Do not repeat the same source photo at several sizes unless a detail crop is pedagogically meaningful.
8. Photography should look contemporary but not trend-dependent enough to date the book quickly.

## Crop families

- **Hero wide:** 16:7 or similar; room for text overlay or strong contextual scene.
- **Editorial landscape:** 4:3; reading/profile/context.
- **Portrait:** 3:4; person-centered feature.
- **Square/detail:** 1:1; objects, food, accessories, signs.
- **Strip:** flexible 2–4 equal crops for matching or sequencing.
- **Inset:** small secondary crop used only when it adds information.

## Overlay rules

Text may sit directly over photography only when:

- the photo has adequate tonal/negative space;
- the text remains readable at print size;
- a functional scrim is used when necessary;
- the overlay does not hide the pedagogically important part of the image.

Do not use gradients merely to decorate a page. Scrims exist only for text legibility.

## People

- Use age-neutral or mixed-age photography appropriate to mixed Brighton learners.
- Avoid exaggerated stock-photo expressions unless the activity genuinely needs a clear emotion.
- Avoid artificial corporate handshakes, staged diversity tableaux, and generic office-stock clichés when more natural photography is available.
- For occupations, show the person meaningfully doing the job when possible.

## Real places / travel

Use recognizable environmental cues when the syllabus names a real city, country, transport context, mall, neighborhood, or landmark. Do not imply a location through generic imagery when a properly licensed real image is available.

## Real celebrities

Only very widely known public figures should appear, and only where pedagogically useful. Record source/license status with the same rigor as any external image.

## Technical requirements

- Production assets are local; never hotlink.
- Minimum effective resolution target: **300 ppi at final print size** when practical; never knowingly use a visibly low-resolution image.
- Preserve the original source file separately from edited/cropped production exports.
- Preferred production formats: WebP/JPEG for photography, PNG when transparency or lossless raster detail is necessary.
- Final crop/position should be controlled by HTML/CSS rather than destructive cropping whenever feasible.
- Record source, creator, license, acquisition date, edits and crop intent in the asset ledger.

## Placeholder rule

Development placeholders must state the intended asset and crop, for example:

`Toronto skyline + traveler · wide hero · subject right · text-safe space left`

This ensures sourcing follows the layout rather than forcing the layout to accommodate a random image later.
