# 6A - City life or country life?

Language-led A lesson: Places in town 2. Printed pages 49-50. No new grammar rule: consolidate be/have, present simple, wants/needs and reasons through the new lexical set. Do not pre-teach the room/preposition syllabus of 6B, movement/directions in 6C or ability can/cannot in 6D.

1. Personal answers. New York is the big-city photograph; Stowe is the small-town photograph. Neither photograph is AI-generated. Students may prefer either; there is no correct lifestyle or stereotype about which is better.
2. Track 6.1: 1 museum F; 2 hospital D; 3 bank A; 4 train station E; 5 post office C; 6 pharmacy B. Photograph signs intentionally support initial word recognition; later tasks require meaning and retrieval. The six-place family is generated generic US imagery, not photographs of these specific places in New York or Stowe.
3. Track 6.2: 1 New York; 2 Stowe. The Brooklyn Transit Museum is in a decommissioned subway station; it is not the Grand Central photograph on page 50. Both descriptions are current factual summaries, not fabricated resident interviews. Real source names and links appear on the page. Stowe's library/museums and New York's park counter the simplistic city=culture/country=no-services contrast. No absent service, comparative adjective, population, price, timetable or actual resident habit is invented.
4. 1 train station; 2 hospital; 3 post office; 4 pharmacy; 5 museum; 6 bank. Accept articles or no article for the place labels. The functional vocabulary panel supplies new chunks send a letter and buy medicine; explain them through the visible envelope/pharmacy scenes before the task. A doctor could work elsewhere in real life; hospital is the intended answer within the explicit six-place set, not a universal claim.
5. Track 6.3: 1 museum; 2 bank; 3 post office. Three fictional people are planning where to go after arriving by train. They describe a purpose without saying the destination. The real Grand Central photograph establishes that scenario; stock travelers are not identified as the recorded speakers. The new picture is distinct from both the city street and the initial station vocabulary scene. Adeline / Mark Natural / Juniper supply the three separate voices; Jessica narrates all labels.
6. Open decision and labeled drawing. Students negotiate any three suitable places; accept earlier vocabulary (library, market, supermarket, cafe, park) as well as this set. Their unknown preferences and reasons become a shared decision, not a pretend hidden-answer grid. A town center is only part of a town; choosing three places does not assert that the entire town has no hospital or other essential services. Use the visible short model, then allow independent reasons and choices. Tell another pair; do not require public posting.

Extra Practice: identify three real local establishments and write their real names and one useful action at each. No purchase, upload or publication is required.
