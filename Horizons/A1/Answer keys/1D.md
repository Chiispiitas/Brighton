# 1D — Let's fill in a form

Student's Book pages 11–12. Editorial edition, 31 October 2026.

1. Emma Carter; Canada; Toronto; 416 555 0182; emma.carter@email.com. Receptionist Lisa asks the questions. These are fictional practice details.
2. 1 What is your last name? 2 Where are you from? 3 Yes: capital letters in Emma, Carter, Canada and Toronto. 4 No capital letter is needed in the displayed email address; copy the address accurately. In ordinary practice, preserve the address the owner gives rather than changing it as prose.
3. 1 Emma Carter; 2 Manta, Ecuador; 3 Sofia Lopez; 4 My name is Daniel.; 5 I am from Canada.; 6 I am in Toronto.
4. 1 c, city; 2 a, phone number; 3 b, email. This short matching task replaces the six-item duplicate check so the complete recorded dialogue can be read at 9.5pt.
5. Choose a registration setting and write an original name for it. Gym/hotel/English school/club or another familiar idea is acceptable.
6. Choose five or six fields relevant to the setting. Personal choices are not a single-answer test.
7. Prepare real or invented details privately. Receptionist asks, writes and checks spelling; visitor answers. Change roles. The completed form must agree with the information the visitor intended. Nobody must provide a real phone number or email.
8. Read the form back together, verify names and spelling/place capitals, and correct errors. The printed Carter exchange is a model, not a compulsory answer. Both learners should finish with an accurate form.

Extra Practice follows the printed prompt. Source dialogue, recording and baseline hotel/form pictures remain. The writing artifact's white response space is retained. The enlarged source, short matching check, private preparation and read-back check strengthen the writing/speaking outcome.
