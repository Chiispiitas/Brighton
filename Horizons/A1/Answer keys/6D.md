# 6D - Where's the mall?

Speaking D: help a visitor choose two places and explain what to do/how to get there. Can/can't supports the conversation and describes available activities and personal availability. It is not another reading-led B lesson or a cashier transaction. Printed pages 55-56.

1. Track 6.10: 1 waterfall A; 2 garden A; 3 roller coaster B; 4 mall both. A is Jewel Changi Airport, Singapore; B is Mall of America, Bloomington, Minnesota, USA. Both are shopping centers with shops. The attraction photos genuinely depict the named places. Garden and waterfall are visible at Jewel; the roller-coaster track is visible inside Mall of America. The photo is historical, so current venue facts are checked independently. Do not invent ticket prices, current opening hours or a claim that every attraction is free. The material does not claim that either venue lacks all the other attraction types.
2. Track 6.11: Sunday. Three fictional friends discuss Jewel; Dan says Grace works on Saturdays and is free on Sunday. Eva/Adeline and Dan/Mark Natural are fictional speaker assignments, not attributed real-person quotations. Dan is not the earlier Tom character, whose established voice is retained. Grace has no recorded speech. Positive can concerns a possible activity at Jewel; I can / Grace can't concerns availability for this particular visit.
3. 1 can't; 2 you can walk. Evidence before clarification. Can is followed by the base verb and does not gain -s with he/she. Question order is Can + subject + verb, with no do/does. Can't is the normal negative; the two contrasting availability sentences, question/short answers and wrong-to-right examples make this visible at 9.5pt. No black bold in the focus box.
4. 1 see; 2 come; 3 can't; 4 can. The personal sentence is open: accept any true can/can't statement within the current language. Do not require students to claim a skill or travel opportunity they do not have.
5. Track 6.12: 1 No / No, it isn't (the museum is closed on Mondays); 2 the park; 3 the library. The café is inside the park. The photo is a fictional information-counter scene and the dialogue belongs to another generic practice city, not to Jewel/Mall of America or the exact 6C map. The transcript has the visitor explicitly choose the park. No official real-city route/opening-day claim is made. The helper says walk straight and turn left. Closed is visibly glossed as not open. All needed answers are in the recording rather than the picture.
6. A chooses two private interests. B prepares three places from their own city and things to do there. Two open lines for interests and three for places preserve preparation space. The shared page contains the roles but none of the partners' actual choices/answers; no fake information gap. Use a city known to B or an imagined town if necessary. Suggested activity chunks recycle park/shopping/art/movie/food; no new tense is required.
7. Real outcome: agree two places for a day, ask what a visitor can do and how to get there, and enter a usable short plan. Models support the exchange; they do not prescribe the partners' selected destinations. Two stop cards give each place a full activity line and two7mm direction lines. Change roles and try another plan. The destination/capability exchange is the speaking goal; the guide is a memory aid, not a writing-skills replacement. Encourage at least one genuine constraint if applicable (e.g., We can't go on Monday; the museum is closed), without inventing negative claims about the city. Do not require can/can't to describe safety, laws or medical capacity.

Extra Practice: record a short private welcome message giving two local activities and directions to one place. A live spoken message is an alternative if recording is unavailable. No upload/publication is required.
