5. A real-object memory game, demonstrated by the teacher. Choose five familiar objects or sets (keys/headphones count as one vocabulary item). Student A looks; while A looks away, B conceals one item. A looks at the four remaining items and guesses the absent one, then B reveals it with This is my ... / These are my ... . Swap roles. A plural set needs Are they ...? and These are ..., not Is it keys? ## Teaching boundaries

Match and personalise the vocabulary, encounter the dialogue, compare near/far singular/plural models, complete controlled image/aural practice, then use retrieval to identify an unknown missing item. Demonstrate the physical routine rather than adding unfamiliar procedural language to the learner page. The missing object is genuinely unknown to the guesser; it is not a printed answer visible to both partners.

Do not introduce present-simple do/does questions, colour adjectives from 2D, possessive pronouns mine/yours, or past-tense lost-object narratives. Names, is/are questions and personal possessive adjectives recycle earlier language. The grammar body retains the approved normal lesson typography and pink shell; the image-generation cutout on page 13 stays transparent. The restored dialogue surface and full photographic backgrounds remain purposeful.
