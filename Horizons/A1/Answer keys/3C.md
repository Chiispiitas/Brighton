# 3C - Where do you go?

Unit 3: My Life. Focus: present-simple questions with where, when, who ... with and what time; adjectives for places. Builds on 3A affirmative routines, 3B do/does, days of the week, 2B times, and 2D adjective position. Does not introduce frequency adverbs, before/after, why/because or a general places-in-town syllabus.

## Answers

1. Palm Park **A**; Beat Café **B**; Cloud Café **C**. The café price labels provide exact evidence for B/C; do not infer price from décor or assume a photograph proves sound levels.
2. beautiful, quiet, busy, noisy, cheap, expensive. Learners locate all six in the reviews. Quiet/noisy and cheap/expensive are opposite pairs; beautiful and busy are not opposites. Use the empty park, people in the café and the explicit $2/$8 prices as context. Briefly clarify the new target meanings in the learners' language where needed. There is no universal absolute threshold for “cheap” or “expensive”; these two prices establish the classroom comparison.
3. Ravi goes to **Cloud Café**.
4. 1 **Where**; 2 **Who**; 3 **When**; 4 **What time**. Students select the question word using the visible answer, not an abstract grammar label.
5. 1 **Where**; 2 **When**; 3 **Who**; 4 **What time**. Capitalize the start of each written question.
6. 1 Where do you go? 2 When does Lena go? 3 Who does he go with? 4 What time do they go?
7. Personal answers about one person in the classmate's family. Write that person's name or relationship in the Person line, then ask where, when, what time and who they go with. The information comes from the partner, not a printed A/B answer table. Suggested questions: Where does your sister go? When does she go? What time does she go? Who does she go with? Adapt he/she and the relationship to the chosen person. Accept short or complete answers. If exact family details are unknown, the partner can choose another person or invent a consistent fictional routine.
8. An independently worded personal place review. Four full writing lines allow students to choose what to say rather than complete sentence stems. Encourage a place description with suitable adjectives and any useful details about visits, companions, day or time; do not require one fixed structure or all those details. Accept a positive, mixed or negative review. The three reviews on page 27 supply models. Example only: Central Park is beautiful and quiet. I go to Central Park on Sunday with my family. We go at nine. This is one possible response, not a compulsory frame.
Extra Practice: personal answers. Ask a family member the two printed questions and write the answers.

## Teaching sequence

The review feature introduces target adjectives in a recognizable setting. Locate and pronounce them in activity 2, then use the Adjectives vocabulary reference for further practice. Recycle adjective location from 2D when needed. Use the 3.7 conversation as direct evidence for question words and do/does. Read it once for Ravi's place, then connect each question to its answer in activity 4. The grammar focus on page 28 clarifies the pattern; activities 5 and 6 move from meaning to word order. Adjective location and a/an can be recycled orally from 2D while checking the reviews. The activity 7 personal information gap gives learners a reason to ask third-person questions: each partner knows a family member's visits, while the page contains no preset answers. The final review transfers the language to a place they actually go to.

Only the four supported question frames are production targets. Who does ... go with? asks about a companion; do not broaden this into subject questions such as Who goes...? Avoid formula-like discovery prompts. Use the visible sentences, bold forms and answers.
