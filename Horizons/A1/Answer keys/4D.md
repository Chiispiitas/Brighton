# 4D - Food for everyone!

**Role:** writing-led D lesson. Outcome: a useful food-sharing post and a partner's reply. Seven activities; printed pages 37-38. Recycle Unit 4 food/countability/quantity language; teach because, so and basic punctuation in this writing context.

## 1 - Sharing food

Track 4.7: **share food; free; waste food**. No numbered matching answers. Read the printed glosses; free means no money, not free time. Explain waste as failing to use/share food while it is still good, not simply food that has not yet been eaten. The model photograph shows four red apples ready to share. Do not introduce a fruit-count problem or new food vocabulary.

## 2 - Two posts

**B** gives all three requested details: **four apples**, **Park Café**, **from four to five**. A only says “food” and gives neither place nor time. Both are deliberately original alternative versions of Nora's same offer. Track 4.8 reads B only; Jessica announces it and Adeline reads Nora, retaining the established 2A voice.

The picture supports the four-apple offer. It does not prove the time, place name or why Nora shares food; these come from the printed/recorded post. The tiny real-life connection is independently sourced, not part of the recording. App layout and posts are fictional, not an Olio screen or real user story.

## 3 - Notice the meaning

1. **Because she doesn't need them.** Accept **because she has too many apples** or **because she doesn't want to waste food** as a sensible inference; only the first is the exact explicit reason in the model. A shorter **She doesn't need them** is fine; do not penalize learners for not producing third-person negatives before that pattern is taught. **I don't need them**, quoted from Nora, is also fine.
2. **Four apples.**

The first answer directs attention to the reason following because. The second identifies the result following so. Read the two original complete sentences before using the Skills box. **Because** gives the reason; **so** gives the result. The comma instruction applies to these two-complete-clause models; it is not a universal rule about every use of so. Initial because clauses are outside this lesson.

## 4 - Punctuation

**Hello. I have too much bread. Do you want it?** Accept **Hello!** for the greeting as a valid alternative, while pointing out the instruction's full-stop model. Capital H, I and D; a sentence boundary after Hello and bread; final question mark. Capital I is independent of position. Names take capitals. The input deliberately lacks punctuation; it is not a model to reproduce unchanged.

## 5 - Because or so

1. I share my bread **because** I have too much.
2. I have too many bananas**, so** I share them.
3. I don't like milk**, so** I share it.

Comma before so in 2 and 3. No added comma required before because in 1. Keep the printed choice options lowercase, italic and normal weight.

## 6 - My post

Open answer. Learners choose a plausible shareable food from the photo or their own idea, not a teacher-supplied private role card. There are eight open writing lines beside the photograph and planning questions, with no compulsory sentence gaps. The visible planning questions support food/quantity, reason, place and time. Ask for a meaningful because sentence and a meaningful so sentence with the taught punctuation. Use the model as support without requiring identical food or reasons.

Possible answer: **Hi! I'm Alex. I have too much bread, so this bread is free. I share it because I don't need it. I'm at Green Café from five to six. Do you want it?**

Evaluate usefulness, clear meaning, connectors, countable/uncountable choices, capital letters and sentence endings. Do not penalize untaught grammar or demand an exact word count. The photograph is an idea showing food sharing, not evidence for a fixed amount, person's name or appointment time.

## 7 - A real reader

Open reply. A partner checks that the post actually contains food, place and time, then states what they want and asks one additional question. The model **I want the bread. Do you have cheese, too?** adds a question rather than asking for information already supplied. Learners adapt the question if their partner already mentions cheese. This is peer response to an independently written post, not a false hidden-information exercise. No actual app account or public posting is required.

## Extra Practice

Open household reflection and one concrete way to use or share food. Possible answer: **I waste bread at home. I don't need it, so I share it with my family.** Accept a short feasible plan in the language learners know. This is not a recipe task repeated from 4C.
